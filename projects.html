Postdoctoral fellowship

CFD modelling of rotating mixing devices using HPC matrix-free algorithms

Rotating mixing devices are an example of process intensification (PI) technology, whose goal is to scale up reactions and hence reduce equipment footprint, energy consumption, and greenhouse gas emissions. My current research at Polytechnique Montréal involves developing high-performance computing (HPC) CFD models to simulate rotating devices, coupling turbulence models and domain decomposition methods through matrix-free algorithms. This is a novel approach that combines the accuracy of the mortar element method, a sliding mesh domain decomposition scheme, with the efficiency of matrix-free algorithms that explore modern hardware capabilities through floating point operations and vector-matrix multiplication in cell batches. I am also employing this novel approach to passive particle transport in mixing devices, with applications to lipid nanoparticle production using population balance equations to describe particle distributions. Representing particle transport through its moments is a novel approach in the fluid dynamics context which allows large-scale coupled particle-fluid simulations without a prohibitively expensive computational cost.

REDEVELOP Challenge

Economic and technical viability of salt cavern Compressed Air Energy Storage in Canada

In the REDEVELOP Challenge 2022, five groups of students from various Canadian universities focused on developing research on clean energy technologies and its integration with Indigenous communities. The research topic of my team was compressed air energy storage (CAES), and our research led to the elaboration of a policy paper, a conference abstract, and a conference poster. We also presented our findings to the Cold Lake First Nations through a virtual meeting. We had various meetings with industry representatives and academia members, aiming to expand our knowledge on the CAES technology. Our biggest contribution was to be able to share our findings with the Cold Lake First Nations, since a new CAES project was to be implemented in their region. We were grateful to partner with energy companies and contribute to the community's understanding of the technology. Our findings were also presented in the Geoconvention 2022 conference.

Conference presentation

Comparison between Alberta's internal load and the electricity generationby hydraulic, solar, and wind sources; results were normalized based on the highest value of each interval. As observed, internal load is not always matched by the energy supplied by renewable sources. Aiming to increase the participation of such energy sources into the grid and still match the internal load, energy storage becomes fundamental to circumvent grid instabilities caused by intermittency of renewable systems. Data was collected from here.

A CAES system has three main components: compressor, air storage reservoir, and expander. During off peak hours, excess energy from the grid is converted into mechanical energy in the compression train. The resulting compressed air is stored in a reservoir (salt cavern). Then, during peak hours, the air is released through the expansion train, where mechanical conversion takes place again to move the turbines and generate electricity to supplement the grid. Video presented by the CAES team at the REDEVELOP Challenge conference (2022).

The REDEVELOP program is an NSERC-funded project that aimed to create multi-university, interdisciplinary teams tasked with finding solutions to scientific questions and/or problems impacting Canada's energy sector. In each edition, an eigth-month training period ended in a conference in Calgary (Alberta) where each team had to present five deliverables: a scientific poster, a 90s video for a general audience, a 15min presentation for a technical audience, and a policy paper. In its 2022 edition, four topics were addressed: CAES, methane emissions, geothermal energy, ans carbon capture and storage.
As the project manager of the CAES team, our group was composed by Jesus R. Parra, Sarah Hasan, Dalila Caparroz, and Aref Rizehbandi.
