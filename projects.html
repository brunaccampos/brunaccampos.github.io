REDEVELOP Challenge

Economic and technical viability of salt cavern Compressed Air Energy Storage in Canada

In the REDEVELOP Challenge 2022, five groups of students from various Canadian universities focused on developing research on clean energy technologies and its integration with Indigenous communities. The research topic of my team was compressed air energy storage (CAES), and our research led to the elaboration of a policy paper, a conference abstract, and a conference poster. We also presented our findings to the Cold Lake First Nations through a virtual meeting. We had various meetings with industry representatives and academia members, aiming to expand our knowledge on the CAES technology. Our biggest contribution was to be able to share our findings with the Cold Lake First Nations, since a new CAES project was to be implemented in their region. We were grateful to partner with energy companies and contribute to the community's understanding of the technology. Our findings were also presented in the Geoconvention 2022 conference.

Comparison between Alberta's internal load and the electricity generationby hydraulic, solar, and wind sources; results were normalized based on the highest value of each interval. As observed, internal load is not always matched by the energy supplied by renewable sources. Aiming to increase the participation of such energy sources into the grid and still match the internal load, energy storage becomes fundamental to circumvent grid instabilities caused by intermittency of renewable systems. Data was collected from here.

A CAES system has three main components: compressor, air storage reservoir, and expander. During off peak hours, excess energy from the grid is converted into mechanical energy in the compression train. The resulting compressed air is stored in a reservoir (salt cavern). Then, during peak hours, the air is released through the expansion train, where mechanical conversion takes place again to move the turbines and generate electricity to supplement the grid. The REDEVELOP program is an NSERC-funded project that aimed to create multi-university, interdisciplinary teams tasked with finding solutions to scientific questions and/or problems impacting Canada's energy sector. In each edition, an eigth-month training period ended in a conference in Calgary (Alberta) where each team had to present five deliverables: a scientific poster, a 90s video for a general audience, a 15min presentation for a technical audience, and a policy paper. In its 2022 edition, four topics were addressed: CAES, methane emissions, geothermal energy, ans carbon capture and storage.

My team won the REDEVELOP Challenge 2022!

As the project manager, I coordinated our team composed by Jesus R. Parra, Sarah Hasan, Dalila Caparroz, and Aref Rizehbandi.
