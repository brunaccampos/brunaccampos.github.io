PhD, Civil and Environmental Engineering

  • University of Waterloo, Canada, 2021-2024
  • Thesis: Finite Element Models for Multiscale Porous Media
  • Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Robert Gracie

Porous media consist of a solid matrix and interconnected pores, and their behavior is commonly described through macroscopic models like Biot (Bt) theory or mixture-based approaches such as the de la Cruz-Spanos (dCS) model. My PhD research comprised developing the first comprehensive finite element formulations for the dCS theory, addressing consolidation and wave propagation in saturated rocks. The work showed that FE convergence depends on solid-fluid coupling, that porosity-related micro-heterogeneity significantly alters wave attenuation, especially for viscous fluids, and that discrepancies between Bt and dCS predictions vary with material properties and frequency. Applications to layered geological domains and core samples demonstrated the importance of the dCS slow S-wave and highlighted how the dCS formulation captures high-frequency and laboratory-scale phenomena more accurately than Biot's model.

MASc, Structural Engineering

  • Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG), Brazil, 2018-2020
  • Thesis: Numerical Integration by Subdivision of Elements using the GFEM
  • Supervisor: Prof.Dr. Felicio Bruzzi Barros

My Master's degree comprised the employment of alternative numerical integration strategies over the conventional ones to be applied in the Generalized Finite Element Method context, specifically in two-dimensional Fracture Mechanics models. I used the subdivision of elements and mapping of the integration points in mesh elements containing discontinuities or singularities, therefore representing the displacements and stress fields more precisely. The implemented strategies were validated by analysing the strain energy and stiffness computed in a range of examples, comparing them to conventional Gauss quadrature situations. The cited strategies were also applied in cases modelled by the Stable Generalized Finite Element Method (GFEM), the global-local methodology (GFEM gl) and the Physically Non-Linear Analysis. Good results were obtained, reducing processing times due to the decrease in the number of integration points, which are allocated taking into account problems particularities. Computational implementation was done in the open-source software INSANE (INteractive Structural ANalysis Environment).

BASc, Civil Engineering

  • University of Uberlândia (UFU), Brazil, 2012-2017
  • Thesis: Structural solutions for concrete slabs with high conduit concentration
  • Supervisor: Maria Cristina Vidigal Lima

The graduation project aimed to study possible structural solutions for the design of solid reinforced-concrete slabs when the effect of a high concentration of electrical conduits is taken into account. Situations like this are recurrent, especially in multi-story residential buildings. They result in a reduction of the concrete's load-resisting section, which may generate unforeseen stresses and make the deformation state critical. The TQS computational program was used to model different scenarios for the typical floor of a previously selected building, varying slab thickness, boundary conditions, and support conditions of the elements that make up the conventional structural system. The data were later compared, with the aid of electronic spreadsheets, to determine the best solution to adopt from both constructive and structural points of view. Finally, additional options for the presented problem were discussed beyond those previously mentioned, such as the use of auxiliary items for electrical conduits in the electrical design, and the adoption of ribbed, trussed, or precast slabs.
